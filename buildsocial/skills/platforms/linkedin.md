## Platform: LinkedIn

- **Voice: the brand's own voice, always.** LinkedIn changes the format, not the personality. A warm, friendly consumer brand stays warm and friendly here (and keeps its language, e.g. a light Hinglish touch); it does not turn into a corporate "thought leader". Never open with lines like "Running a brand means..." or "As a company, we...".
- **Sound like the example posts.** Same sentence length, same warmth, same Hindi-English mix. LinkedIn posts can be a little longer, but they should still read as if the same person wrote them.
- **Keep the brand's language mix exact.** "English with a light Hinglish touch" means mostly English sentences with a few Hindi words or one short Hindi phrase, not sentences that are mostly Hindi. Plain "Hinglish" can mix more.
- **The product part uses only approved claims.** When you name what's in the product, use only the approved claims, even if the research or ingredient list mentions other things.
- **What makes it worth reading:** one practical thing the reader can use (a tip, why something happens, a small mistake to avoid), said the way a friend would say it, then a natural link to the product or news. Use only the one or two facts that serve this; you don't have to use every fact.
- **Not a market report.** For consumer brands, talk to the reader about their own life, not about the industry. Never write sentences about trends, the category or what "consumers" or "people in the city" prefer (e.g. "X is growing in popularity", "many consumers prefer..."), even if the research says so.
- **No invented stories or observations.** Don't make up anecdotes, team moments, numbers or "we noticed..." statements. If it isn't in the brand profile, the brief or the facts, leave it out.
- **Hook:** the first one or two lines show before "...more". Open with the insight or a relatable situation from the reader's life, not with the brand or the product.
- **Structure:** short paragraphs of one to three sentences, with a blank line between them. One idea per post.
- **Emojis:** none, or one at most.
- **Hashtags:** 2 to 4, specific to the topic, only in the hashtags field.
- **Call to action:** one, at the end: usually a question readers can answer from their own experience. Don't add a second request (like "visit our page") after it.
- **No Instagram conventions:** LinkedIn has no "link in bio". Point people to the company page, a named resource, or the comments instead.
